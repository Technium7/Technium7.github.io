About me (contact)

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Cornell (M.S., December 2025), working in Prof. Karan Mehta’s group on trapped-ion quantum computing and on robots that automate precision work in the lab.

My work spans end-to-end experiment and system design: building cryogenic and ultra-high-vacuum apparatus, developing optical and electrical control stacks, and writing software that accelerates experiment design, calibration, and operation. I am also interested in automation with robots and have automated experiment preparations such as photonics packaging and optical path maintenance with a robotic arm in our working lab.

Selected Projects

Robotics

Compressed-Memory Retrieval for VLA Steering

Designed a retrieval method that compresses a frozen SmolVLA vision-language model’s 960-dimensional output (1.3 KB) into 64-dimensional keys (128 bytes). The compressor is trained with a soft dynamic time-warping contrastive loss based on action similarity. With a 300-episode memory (1.5 MB), direct-rollout success on initial tests rose from 33% to 62% across 100 held-out peg-insertion tasks. Manuscript in preparation.

Robotic Automation of Precision Optics-Lab Operations

Built and deployed a low-cost tabletop robotic arm that automates sensor manipulation, optical power measurement, and photonic-chip packaging in the existing lab environment, using only one additional top-view camera. The perception, control, and motion stack allows for 0.95 mm localization uncertainty and a task-specific accuracy of 0.2 mm or better. Submitted to ICRA 2027.

Quantum Science

Ion-Trap Junction and Transport Co-Optimization

Co-optimized junction electrodes and transport trajectories with a Bayesian neural network and the cross-entropy method, reducing the final phonon number by about 100× for high-speed transport under noise. Developed a quadratic-programming solver for dynamic multi-voltage control that lowers transport complexity from O(nm) to O(n). Those voltage solutions were used to demonstrate ion transport on chips with multilayer integrated photonics for the first time. Manuscript in preparation.

Rapid Multi-Mode Trapped-Ion Laser Cooling

Demonstrated laser-cooling schemes that reduce total cooling time by more than 10×, addressing the runtime bottleneck in trapped-ion quantum computing, where cooling dominates operating time. The experimental and theoretical results are published in PRX Quantum and Physical Review Applied, respectively.

Building an AMO Lab for Trapped-Ion Quantum Computing: Apparatus and Control

Designed and built a trapped-ion system, as a founding student in the lab, that includes multistage cryogenic vacuum chambers, DC and RF filtering and stabilization, and free-space, fiber, and integrated-photonics delivery for narrow-linewidth UV–IR lasers, with FPGA and DAC control.

Developed Python packages that speed up ion-trap chip design and automate calibration, data collection, and in-situ analysis. Several are now used by research groups at ETH, Duke, and UCB.
